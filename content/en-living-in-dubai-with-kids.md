## Pregnancy and giving birth in Dubai

### When to register your pregnancy

Most women register with a clinic at around 6 weeks. That leaves enough time for all the screenings and tests.

### Documents to bring

The clinic will ask for your passport, residence visa and Emirates ID (originals and copies), plus your insurance card. If you have medical records or reports from previous gynaecologist visits, bring those too.

Health insurance is mandatory for all UAE residents, but not every policy covers pregnancy and delivery. Check this before your first appointment.

### Maternity insurance in the UAE

You can get a policy through one parent's employer or buy one directly from an insurer.

If you already have insurance, check exactly what it covers. Some policies pay for check-ups and tests but not the delivery itself. In that case, upgrade to a wider plan.

If you're already pregnant and don't have insurance yet, the insurer will ask for a doctor's letter confirming the pregnancy.

We recommend the widest policy your budget allows. Apart from tests and scans, it usually includes:

- a list of hospitals where you can give birth;
- natural delivery and C-section;
- epidural anaesthesia;
- some medication;
- cover for the baby in the first months of life.

Mind the waiting period. Maternity cover doesn't start the day you buy the policy: most insurers set a waiting period of 6 to 12 months. There's no single DHA rule on this, each company sets its own, and some waive it for an extra premium. That's why it makes sense to buy maternity insurance while you're still planning a pregnancy.

Newborns are a different story: in Dubai, insurers are not allowed to apply a waiting period to babies, so you can insure your child right after birth.

### What to pack for the hospital

The hospital provides everything you need for the birth and the baby's first days, including baby clothes and toiletries. You only need your personal things: phone, chargers, toiletries and going-home outfits for you and the baby. Don't forget your passport, Emirates ID, insurance card and medical records.

### Giving birth

Go to the hospital when your waters break or contractions come about every 10 minutes. Don't wait much longer, especially as home births are not permitted in the UAE. If labour is fast and you can't get there in time, call an ambulance: the crew will take care of you and the baby.

You can have your husband or a doula with you. A doula gives emotional and physical support but doesn't give medical advice.

### Going home

If everything is fine, mothers are usually discharged 1-2 days after a natural birth and 3-4 days after a C-section. The doctor decides the exact timing. During your stay the baby is examined, you get advice on newborn care and a schedule of check-ups.

When you leave, the hospital gives you a birth notification. You must register the birth within 30 days, or you'll be fined. You have 120 days for the baby's other documents: passport, residence visa and Emirates ID.

### Birth certificate in Dubai

You'll need:

- both parents' passports with valid residence visas;
- both parents' Emirates IDs;
- the hospital birth notification;
- your marriage certificate. If you married outside the UAE, it must be attested by the UAE embassy and the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs and translated into Arabic.

In Dubai, birth certificates are issued by the Dubai Health Authority (DHA). You apply online and sign in with UAE Pass. In other emirates, the Ministry of Health handles it.

Your home country's birth certificate is issued by its consulate in Dubai. Russian citizens, for example, apply to the Russian consulate with the same set of documents and an application form.

A child gets UAE citizenship only if the father is a UAE national.

## Nursery in Dubai

Nurseries in Dubai accept babies from 45 days old. Parents decide how many hours a day the child spends there.

Groups are usually split by age:

1. Nursery: from 45 days to 3 years.
2. FS1 or KG1: up to about 4.5 years. First steps in independence and early learning activities.
3. FS2 or KG2: the pre-school year. These groups are often attached to a school the child will later join. Children start learning letters, numbers and writing.

### Early years curricula

Approaches vary a lot between nurseries, so it's worth understanding them before you choose.

The most common in Dubai is the British EYFS (Early Years Foundation Stage). Children learn through play: speaking, reading, numbers, creativity, physical and emotional development. It's built on respect for each child as they are.

NAEYC is often taken for a teaching method, but it's an American association that sets quality standards for early education: development, safety and health. A NAEYC-accredited nursery has been checked against those standards.

In Montessori nurseries, children choose their own activities in a specially prepared environment where they can do everything without adult help. The teacher observes and guides rather than leads.

Reggio Emilia is built around what interests the children: exploration, projects and a lot of creative work.

### Nursery documents

For the child: birth certificate, passport, Emirates ID, photo, health insurance and vaccination record.

For the mother: passport, Emirates ID, residence visa and photo. The same set is required from the sponsor, the person paying the fees.

### Good to know

Many nurseries don't provide meals, so you'll pack a lunchbox. Ask about this right away.

Most nurseries are open from 7 am to 6 pm, and many close for the summer holidays.

Late pick-ups are taken seriously. If you don't collect your child on time, you may be fined.

## Schools in Dubai

Choosing a school deserves time. The quality of education here is good, but the systems differ a lot from what families from Russia and the CIS are used to. School is also a child's first real community, and the friends they make there often stay for years.

### Key facts

Education in the UAE is compulsory from age 6. Start looking early: popular schools have limited places and entrance assessments. Getting in is easier if your child already attends the school's nursery.

The school year usually starts at the end of August and ends in June or July, depending on the curriculum. Most schools offer meals, but children can bring food from home.

### British, American or IB curriculum

Start with the curriculum: it determines which universities your child can apply to later.

The British curriculum is the most common in Dubai. Students take IGCSEs at 16 and A-Levels at 18, which open the door to British and many other universities.

The American curriculum prepares students for US universities. It has core subjects, plenty of electives and several levels of difficulty.

The IB (International Baccalaureate) diploma is recognised by universities worldwide.

Dubai also has schools following Indian, French, German and other curricula.

| Curriculum | Final exams | Easiest university route |
|---|---|---|
| British | IGCSE at 16, A-Level at 18 | UK and many universities worldwide |
| American | High School Diploma, plus SAT or ACT | USA and Canada |
| IB | IB Diploma at 18 | universities worldwide |

### Public or private school

Public schools are run by the UAE Ministry of Education and are free for UAE nationals. Expat children can enrol too, but the conditions are tough: AED 6,000 a year, foreign students make up no more than 20% of places, admission is merit-based and all subjects are taught in Arabic. That's why most expat families choose private schools.

Private schools in Dubai are inspected by KHDA (Knowledge and Human Development Authority). Each school receives one of six ratings: Outstanding, Very Good, Good, Acceptable, Weak or Very Weak. Inspectors look at student progress, teaching, facilities, Arabic and more. You can read the inspection report for any private school on the KHDA website.

Before deciding, visit a few schools on your shortlist, talk to the teachers and look around the campus. And think about the commute. Dubai is big, and an hour in traffic twice a day will wear out both you and your child.

### Moving from a school in another country

For children arriving from Russian-speaking countries, studying in English is usually the hardest part. Assess your child's English level early. If it's still weak, look for a school with English language support (EAL). Your child can catch up without overload and later move to a school with higher entry requirements.

Before the first day, read through the curriculum, the grading system and the list of after-school activities. It'll be easier to support your child when they start telling you about school.

### School admission in Dubai

Many schools accept students throughout the year if there are places in the class. The process usually looks like this:

1. Shortlist schools, check availability and book a tour.
2. Submit an application and gather documents.
3. Pay the registration fee; your child takes an entrance assessment.
4. If all goes well, the school sends an offer. To secure the place, you pay a registration deposit, capped by KHDA at 10% of the annual fee. It's later deducted from tuition.
5. Submit the remaining documents.

Without an attested transfer certificate from the previous school, your child won't be allowed to start classes. A residence visa and Emirates ID are needed before the first day, although you can apply without them.

## Hiring a nanny in Dubai

A good nanny takes care of your child and often some of the housework too: cooking, cleaning, shopping.

### What kind of nanny you need

First decide what you want to hand over. Nannies differ:

- a newborn nanny knows everything about caring for a baby in the first year;
- a nanny for 1-3 year olds manages routine, meals and hygiene and picks age-appropriate games;
- a governess for children aged 3-12 organises activities, helps with homework and builds independence;
- a nanny-housekeeper combines childcare with cleaning and cooking.

Also decide on the schedule, live-in or live-out, and what matters to you, such as speaking Russian or English.

### Documents

From the nanny:

1. Passport and work visa if she isn't a UAE national. The visa is issued under the employer's sponsorship.
2. Police clearance certificate from her country of citizenship or residence.
3. Health insurance and a medical certificate, including an HIV test.
4. A teaching or medical qualification, if she has one: it makes a candidate easier to trust.

From you as the employer:

1. Copies of your passport and residence visa.
2. Proof of income: a salary certificate or bank statement.
3. An employment contract setting out duties, hours, salary and other terms.

### Bringing a nanny from abroad

If you want to bring your own nanny to Dubai, you become her sponsor. Domestic worker visas are processed through the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE), directly or via Tadbeer centres. To sponsor a worker, you need a proven income, usually from AED 6,000 a month, but check the current threshold on the MOHRE website. The nanny's documents must be translated and attested. With a complete file, processing usually takes one to several weeks, depending largely on the medical check.

## FAQ

**Can I give birth in Dubai without insurance?**
Yes, but you'll pay for everything yourself. Most insurers only cover delivery 6-12 months after you buy the policy, so arrange it in advance.

**How long do I have to get a birth certificate in Dubai?**
You must register the birth within 30 days. You have 120 days for the baby's passport, visa and Emirates ID.

**At what age do nurseries in Dubai accept children?**
Nurseries accept babies from 45 days old.

**At what age do children start school in Dubai?**
Education in the UAE is compulsory from age 6. Foundation stages (FS1 and FS2, or KG1 and KG2) start earlier.

**How do I choose a school in Dubai?**
Decide on the curriculum (British, American or IB), read the KHDA inspection report, visit the school and consider the commute and your child's level of English.

**Can expat children attend public schools in the UAE?**
Yes, but it costs AED 6,000 a year, foreign students are limited to 20% of places and all lessons are in Arabic. That's why most families choose private schools.

**How can my child keep up their Russian at an international school?**
Speaking at home is usually not enough; regular lessons with a teacher make the difference. Start with a [free assessment](/dia), and we'll suggest the right programme.
