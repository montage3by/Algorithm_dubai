Parent's guide By the Algorithm Training Center team · Updated 26 November 2026

Your choice of school in Dubai shapes a lot: which language your child will think in a couple of years from now, which universities they can apply to and who their friends will be. And the choice is huge: the emirate has hundreds of private schools teaching a dozen different curricula.

Here's the step-by-step approach that helps families at our centre narrow the list down to two or three schools and get through admissions calmly.

Step 1. Decide which curriculum suits your child

The curriculum matters more than the school's name. It decides the exams, the workload and which universities will be easier to apply to later.

CurriculumFinal examsBest for
BritishIGCSE at 16, A-Levels at 18Families looking at universities in the UK and Europe who like a clear, exam-based system
AmericanHigh School Diploma and GPA, plus SAT or ACT for US universitiesFamilies planning on the US or Canada, and children who work steadily through the year
IBIB Diploma after the last two years (DP)Independent learners: lots of projects, essays and research, recognised by universities worldwide

British curriculum

The most common curriculum in Dubai. Learning is split into Key Stages. Students take IGCSEs at 16 and A-Levels at 18, and these exams are accepted by British and many other universities.

American curriculum

A wide choice of subjects and a cumulative grading system (GPA), ending with a High School Diploma. Students applying to US universities also take the SAT or ACT.

IB programme

The International Baccalaureate has three stages: PYP in primary, MYP in middle school and DP in the last two years. The IB Diploma is recognised by universities around the world. It's considered one of the most demanding programmes, with a lot of research projects, essays and independent work.

Other options

Dubai also has schools with Indian (CBSE, ICSE), French, German and other national curricula. There are very few schools offering the full Russian curriculum, so Russian-speaking families usually choose an international school and keep up the Russian programme alongside it.

Step 2. Check the school's KHDA rating

Private schools in Dubai are regulated by the KHDA (Knowledge and Human Development Authority). After an inspection, each school gets an overall rating on a six-point scale:

Good
Acceptable
Weak
Very Weak
Highest ratingLowest rating

Inspectors look at attainment and progress, quality of teaching, safety, support for children with special needs, and Arabic and Islamic studies.

The KHDA didn't run regular inspections of most schools in 2024-25 and 2025-26, so most schools still carry the rating from their last inspection. Alongside the overall rating, each school's page in the KHDA directory has a short parent summary report on teaching quality and student wellbeing.

How to use the ratings:

  • Read the report, not just the overall rating. A school can be Outstanding for English and Good for maths.
  • Look at the trend. A school that has moved up from Good to Very Good can be more interesting than one that has held the same rating for years.
  • A rating doesn't replace a visit. It won't show you the atmosphere, how teachers treat children or how the school supports pupils for whom English is a second language.

Ratings and reports are published on the KHDA website and app.

Step 3. Think about the commute and costs

Commute. Dubai is big, and a school 40 minutes away means almost an hour and a half in the car every day. Ask whether the school bus serves your area.

Costs. Fees vary a lot from school to school and go up with each year group. On top of tuition fees, budget for the application fee, the seat deposit, uniform, transport, meals and clubs. Schools publish their current fees on their websites.

English support. If your child has just arrived, ask about EAL (English as an Additional Language): how it works, how many hours a week it runs and when children move into mainstream classes.

Other Russian speakers in the class. They make the first months easier. But if there are too many, English may develop more slowly.

Step 4. Book a school tour

Pick three to five schools and book a school tour. Questions worth asking:

  • How many children are in a class, and how many teaching assistants?
  • How does the school support children who don't speak much English yet?
  • How often do parents get feedback?
  • Which clubs are included in the fees, and which cost extra?
  • Are there places in the year group you need, and is there a waiting list?

Watch how teachers talk to children in the corridors. It tells you more than any admissions presentation.

Step 5. Prepare the documents

Each school has its own list, but you'll usually need:

  • an online application and the application fee;
  • the child's and parents' passports;
  • residence visa and Emirates ID: you can apply without them, but they're required before your child starts;
  • birth certificate;
  • photos;
  • school reports from the last one or two years;
  • vaccination record;
  • a Transfer Certificate from the previous school.

A Transfer Certificate is required for children joining Year 3 (Grade 2) and above from a school outside Dubai. If it was issued abroad, it has to be attested in the country of issue and by the UAE embassy. Your child can't start classes without it, so check the exact process with your chosen school early.

Documents in Russian usually need to be translated into English or Arabic.

Step 6. Take the entrance assessment

  1. You submit the application and pay the application fee. Under KHDA rules it can't exceed AED 500 and isn't deducted from tuition.
  2. Your child takes the assessment: English and maths, sometimes an interview. For younger children it's a play-based session, for older ones a written test.
  3. If all goes well, the school sends an offer.
  4. To secure the place, you pay a seat deposit. The KHDA caps it at 10% of the annual tuition fee, and it's then deducted from the fees.

Many schools admit children throughout the year if there are places. Popular schools, though, have waiting lists a year or more ahead, so start early.

Age at entry

From the 2026-27 academic year, children registering at a Dubai school for the first time are placed by their age on 31 December. In schools that start in September, that means FS1 from age 3, FS2 from 4, Year 1 from 5 and Year 2 from 6.

How to prepare your child for admission

Russian-speaking children rarely struggle with the maths. They struggle with English: they don't understand how a task is worded, or get lost in an unfamiliar test format.

What helps:

  1. Check their level early, six months before applying, not one month.
  2. Practise the test format, not just the language: picture-based tasks, logic puzzles, reading short texts against the clock.
  3. Keep maths strong. The Russian curriculum usually gives a solid foundation, and that's a real advantage in the test.

An international school almost always crowds out Russian. After a year or two children still speak it, but reading and writing become hard. If you want to keep the language, or keep the option of going back to a Russian school, the Russian programme needs to run alongside international school.

FAQ

How much does school cost in Dubai?

It depends on the school, curriculum and year group, and fees can differ several times over. Check fees on school websites and budget for extras: application fee, seat deposit, uniform and transport.

When should we apply to a school in Dubai?

For popular schools, a year or more ahead. Many schools also admit children during the year if there are places, but the choice is smaller.

What is a good KHDA rating?

Outstanding and Very Good are the top levels, Good is solidly average. Read the full report: the overall rating doesn't show strengths and weaknesses by subject.

Is there a Russian school in Dubai?

There are very few schools with the full Russian curriculum in Dubai. Many families choose an international school and follow the Russian programme alongside it, for example in a hybrid format with assessments in Dubai.

Does my child need English to get into a Dubai school?

Yes, almost every school tests English. If your child's level is still low, look for schools with EAL support and start preparing early.

How much is the deposit when a school offers a place?

Under KHDA rules the application fee is up to AED 500 and the seat deposit is up to 10% of the annual tuition fee. The deposit is then deducted from the fees.
